====================================================================
MARKT — USER-CENTRED EVALUATION
"The app as it is" vs "what someone would actually want to use"
Date: 2026-09-05
====================================================================

HOW THIS WAS EVALUATED (read this first)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
This is an honest, build-and-flow-grounded review, not a fabricated
click-by-click QA. A true end-to-end live run isn't reliably possible
from here because:
  - the app is gated behind a real backend login that returns 403 until
    an account's email is verified (no verified test account on hand), and
  - Paystack payment has no test credentials wired.
The build and lint are clean (0 errors). Everything below reflects the
ACTUAL implemented behaviour of the code and the gaps already surfaced
during the build — where a claim depends on the backend, it says so.

Severity legend:
  [BLOCKER] a real person likely abandons here
  [MAJOR]   serious friction / trust problem
  [MINOR]   noticeable but livable
  [POLISH]  nice-to-have

--------------------------------------------------------------------
BOTTOM LINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Markt looks and feels like a real product. The UI is clean, on-brand,
consistent, responsive, and the core buyer and seller journeys exist and
are wired to a live API. As a *demo* or *investor walkthrough*, it's
strong.

As something a stranger would rely on to actually buy or sell, it is NOT
there yet — and almost every remaining blocker is on the BACKEND, not the
UI. The three that matter most: (1) people can't trust a purchase they
get no email about, (2) the payment return path isn't proven end-to-end,
(3) status changes don't notify the other party. Fix those and it crosses
from "nice demo" to "usable".

Readiness by journey (quick scorecard):
  Discover / browse ........... GOOD
  Sign up / sign in ........... OK, with a verification-gate friction
  Buy (cart -> checkout) ...... OK, until payment
  Pay & confirm ............... RISKY (backend-dependent)
  Track my order .............. WEAK (no proactive updates)
  Sell / inventory ............ GOOD
  Fulfil orders (seller) ...... OK, but no buyer feedback loop
  Community ................... GOOD
  Chat / messaging ............ OK
  Settings / account .......... GOOD
  Notifications ............... WEAK (in-app only, and under-emitted)

====================================================================
DETAILED FINDINGS BY JOURNEY
====================================================================

1) DISCOVER / BROWSE  — GOOD
   Expect: land somewhere, browse products without signing up, see a
           product's detail, understand price and seller.
   Has:    Root is a public marketplace; product cards open a public
           preview; search, category + sub-category filters; prices in
           naira with separators. Buying prompts sign-in. This is exactly
           the "browse before commit" pattern people expect.
   Gaps:   - [MINOR] Category filter is exact-match: a product only shows
             under its own sub-category chip, not its parent. A shopper who
             clicks the parent may think the shelf is empty.
           - [MINOR] Search quality is entirely backend-driven; relevance
             is unverified.
           - [POLISH] No sort/relevance cues, no "recently viewed", thin
             empty states.

2) SIGN UP / SIGN IN  — OK, with real friction
   Expect: create an account, get in quickly.
   Has:    Full-screen split auth, buyer/seller switcher, a 3-step
           register with live username check, verify-email flow with code
           entry, password reset with code.
   Gaps:   - [MAJOR] Email-verification gate: a new account cannot sign in
             until the email is verified (backend returns 403). If the
             verification email is slow or unbranded (it's not wired to the
             app's own email system), this is where new users bounce. This
             is the single biggest onboarding risk and it's backend-owned.
           - [MINOR] The buyer/seller switcher errors helpfully if you pick
             a role you don't have — good — but there's no one-click "add
             the other role" from that error yet.

3) BUY: CART -> CHECKOUT  — OK
   Expect: add to cart, adjust quantity, enter/confirm address, pay.
   Has:    Cart with quantity stepper and live subtotals; checkout collects
           recipient name (required), phone, and a full address; "use saved
           address" prefills and is now editable; order summary; errors
           surface as toasts.
   Gaps:   - [MINOR] If your saved address is incomplete, you must complete
             it at checkout (now possible since fields are editable) — fine,
             but there's no nudge in Settings to keep the saved address whole.
           - [POLISH] No shipping-cost preview before the summary; the
             10% coupon discount is a flat client-side assumption.

4) PAY & CONFIRM  — RISKY (backend-dependent)  [BLOCKER-class if unfixed]
   Expect: pay, come back to a clear "order confirmed", get a receipt.
   Has:    Paystack initialize -> redirect; a callback_url is set back to
           the order-confirmation page; that page reads ?paid=1 and shows
           "confirmed" or "confirming…" without re-prompting payment.
   Gaps:   - [BLOCKER] Unproven end-to-end. The observed 404 after paying
             came from the backend's Paystack callback redirecting off-app.
             The app now passes a callback_url, but the documented
             /payments/initialize body doesn't officially accept one, so the
             backend may ignore it. Until a real payment round-trips to the
             confirmation page, this is a trust-breaker.
           - [MAJOR] No emailed receipt (see Notifications).

5) TRACK MY ORDER  — WEAK
   Expect: know where my order is WITHOUT going to look; get told when it
           ships.
   Has:    A clean 4-stop journey (Placed/Preparing/Shipped/Delivered),
           items, address, activity, totals. The journey now derives from
           item progress so it advances correctly even when the order-level
           status lags.
   Gaps:   - [MAJOR] Zero proactive updates. When a seller ships, the buyer
             is NOT notified — no email, no push, and (today) no in-app
             notification is even created. A buyer has to keep re-opening
             the order to learn anything. Backend must emit notifications on
             status change.
           - [MINOR] "Delivered" relies on the seller manually marking it;
             the API has a whole rider/POD delivery domain that isn't built
             into the app, so there's no independent delivery confirmation.

6) SELL / INVENTORY  — GOOD
   Expect: add products, see stock and demand, edit fast.
   Has:    Inventory view with stat cards (products, units in stock, low/out
           of stock, units ordered), a product table with stock badges, and
           an Add/Edit-product modal with grouped panels + an image picker.
   Gaps:   - [MINOR] No bulk actions, no low-stock alerting beyond the badge,
             no category shown per row (the product payload doesn't expose it).
           - [POLISH] "Units ordered" is a lifetime count, not a period.

7) FULFIL ORDERS (SELLER)  — OK, but the loop is open
   Expect: see incoming orders, advance them, and have the buyer kept in
           the loop.
   Has:    A seller "Orders to fulfil" list and, on the order detail, a
           Fulfilment panel that advances each item (prepare -> ship ->
           deliver) via the real seller endpoint.
   Gaps:   - [MAJOR] Advancing status doesn't notify the buyer (same
             backend gap as #5). The seller does the right thing and the
             buyer never hears about it.
           - [MINOR] Order-level status can lag item status on the backend;
             the UI compensates by deriving from items, but the underlying
             rollup is worth fixing server-side.

8) COMMUNITY  — GOOD
   Expect: a feed, posts, follow people.
   Has:    Feed, post detail, comments, follow (now seeded from real state
           on the post page).
   Gaps:   - [MINOR] "People to follow" suggestions still show Follow after
             you follow + refresh (no per-suggestion follow flag to seed
             from). Likes/comments now deep-link to the post.

9) CHAT / MESSAGING  — OK
   Has:    Room list, thread view, start-a-chat with a seller.
   Gaps:   - [MINOR] Polling-based; no typing/read receipts; new-message
             notifications depend on the same emit gap.

10) SETTINGS / ACCOUNT  — GOOD
   Has:    One tabbed hub: Profile, Account (buyer/seller roles), Address,
           Billings (wallet balance + transactions), Notifications
           preferences, Privacy, and a real Delete-account (with a
           can-delete check).
   Gaps:   - [MINOR] Billings is read-only — no top-up/withdraw UI yet
             (endpoints exist).

11) NOTIFICATIONS  — WEAK (the connective tissue is missing)
   Expect: get told when things happen — by email at least for money and
           orders.
   Has:    A well-designed in-app notifications page (unread/read tabs,
           search, categories, deep links) and a Notifications preferences
           tab whose choices are saved.
   Gaps:   - [BLOCKER-class] No email delivery at all (in-app + push only),
             and — more fundamentally — many events don't even CREATE an
             in-app notification (order shipped, new order for seller, etc.).
             A spec for the backend is written (docs/backend/
             email-notifications-spec.md) but until the backend emits on
             events and sends email, the app feels silent.

====================================================================
CROSS-CUTTING ISSUES (mostly backend, all trust-related)
====================================================================
  A) [BLOCKER] No transactional email anywhere — no order confirmation,
     no "shipped", no receipt, no verification that feels first-party.
     People do not trust money-in-an-app with zero email trail.
  B) [BLOCKER] Payment round-trip unproven; risk of landing off-app.
  C) [MAJOR]  Events don't reliably notify the other party (buyer<->seller).
  D) [MAJOR/SECURITY] GET /media/ is readable with NO authentication and
     can expose every user's uploaded files. The app scopes what it shows,
     but the endpoint itself must be gated server-side.
  E) [MINOR]  Whole domains exist in the API but not the app yet: delivery/
     rider flow + POD, wallet top-up/withdraw, gamification, seller
     analytics timeseries. Their absence is why "delivered" and payouts
     feel manual.

====================================================================
WHAT'S GENUINELY GOOD (don't lose these)
====================================================================
  - Cohesive, warm, on-brand design system; consistent across ~30 screens;
    light/dark; responsive; naira formatting done centrally.
  - "Browse before sign-up" public marketplace + gated app is the right
    consumer-marketplace shape.
  - Role-awareness is real: buyers and sellers get genuinely different
    dashboards, orders, and tools from ONE account.
  - Honest engineering: errors surface (toasts + dev logs), forms don't
    wipe on error, the architecture (same-origin BFF, session cookie relay,
    server actions) is sound and production-shaped.
  - Inventory + fulfilment + a tabbed settings hub make the seller side feel
    like a real back-office, not a toy.

====================================================================
TOP PRIORITIES TO CLOSE THE GAP (in order)
====================================================================
  1. Make ONE real payment round-trip land on the confirmation page.
     (Backend: honor callback_url or set the Paystack redirect target.)
  2. Emit an in-app notification on every state change, and send the
     matching email — start with order placed / shipped / delivered and
     payment success. (Backend: the spec is written.)
  3. Gate GET /media/ behind auth. (Backend, security.)
  4. Smooth the email-verification onboarding: branded, fast, with a clear
     resend and a "we sent you a link" state that doesn't dead-end.
  5. Prove the seller-ships -> buyer-sees-it loop end-to-end.
  6. Category filter: let a parent category include its sub-categories, or
     always show sub-chips so nothing looks empty.
  7. Wallet top-up/withdraw UI (endpoints already exist).
  8. Add a period to seller metrics and a low-stock alert.
  9. Empty/loading states pass across the app.
 10. A genuine mobile click-through (layouts are responsive but unverified
     on a real device).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE-LINE VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
A well-built, good-looking marketplace whose remaining "would I actually
use this?" gaps are almost entirely backend trust plumbing — email,
payment proof, and notifications — not the interface.
